每日英语口语练习 2026-07-28 (星期二)

📖 原文/对话

来源: BBC 6 Minute English — “Children in warzones” (2026年7月23日) 节目主持人: Neil & Becca 特邀嘉宾: Fergal Keane (BBC战地记者), Asma Khalid

Neil: Hello, this is 6 Minute English from BBC Learning English. I’m Neil.

Becca: And I’m Becca.

Neil: Now, today, we’re going to be talking about the very serious topic of war and how it affects children.

Becca: Some of the discussions you hear will mention death and trauma. We won’t go into detail, but if you think you feel may uncomfortable, you can check the transcript for this episode before listening to it on our website, bbclearningenglish.com.

Neil: Yes. And remember, you’ll find all this episode’s vocabulary, along with a free worksheet, and many other 6 Minute English episodes on our website.

Becca: The most recent figure from 2024 says that 520 million children are living in conflict zones. That’s about one in every five children worldwide.

Neil: In this episode, we’ll hear from Fergal Keane on the BBC World Service Programme The Global Story. Fergal is a BBC reporter known best for reporting from war zones around the world and, as a result, developed PTSD, a type of stress disorder.

Becca: Now, in the clips we’ll hear journalist Asma Khalid asks Fergal Keane about his own experience with childhood trauma. Fergal grew up in a home with an alcoholic father. Asma wanted to know how his own trauma affected his reporting on the experiences of children in warzones:

Fergal Keane: I was hyper-vigilant from the very earliest age, obviously watching out, you know, am I under threat, is there a danger to me here?

Neil: Fergal mentions that he was hyper-vigilant because he was in dangerous situations as a child. The adjective vigilant means to always be careful to notice things, particularly when there is a possibility of danger.

Becca: Yes. And the prefix hyper- expresses that there is a lot of or even too much of something.

Neil: So, hyper-vigilant means very, very careful and watchful. Hypervigilance, the noun, is a state of extreme alertness and sensitivity to surroundings, possibly of extreme anxiety.

Becca: Now let’s hear from Fergal as he talks about research on the experience of children in warzones:

Fergal Keane: I mean, they disagree about a few things, but on this they’re absolutely of one mind. And that is that you can’t recover from war trauma if you’re living with continuing trauma.

Becca: Fergal goes on to talk about some of the ways in which these children might be helped:

Fergal Keane: And so there’s a new field of theory, well I mean relatively new field of theory which encourages children to slowly and carefully try to confront — obviously not death and massacre again — but things like learning to sleep alone, not having to sleep with a parent because that’s what they did during the war because they were so consistently terrified. Now, it sounds like a simple thing, but as anybody who’s tried to put a child to sleep, who’s had a nightmare, a child who hasn’t been traumatised will know it’s very difficult.

Neil: Fergal says that children should be encouraged to slowly and carefully try to confront their experiences. To confront something is to meet with it or deal with it.

Becca: Sleeping alone seems simple enough, but Fergal compares the experiences of a child in a warzone to that of children who have nightmares.

Neil: Now, let’s hear Fergal talk about what he has learned from reporting on children in warzones:

Fergal Keane: The big lesson is that, given a chance, the human spirit, and especially children, are phenomenally resilient.

Becca: Fergal describes the children as being phenomenally resilient. The adverb phenomenally describes something being done or felt in an extreme or surprising way.

Neil: To be resilient is to be able to recover from and come back from something difficult or bad that’s happened. So, Fergal says that children can be surprisingly positive despite the negative things happening around them.

📝 重点词汇

1. hyper-vigilant /ˌhaɪpər ˈvɪdʒɪlənt/ (adj.)

  • 含义: 过度警觉的，高度警惕的
  • 例句: After the accident, she became hyper-vigilant, always checking her surroundings for potential danger.

2. be of one mind /biː ɒv wʌn maɪnd/ (phrase)

  • 含义: 意见一致，达成共识
  • 例句: The researchers disagree about a few things, but on this they’re absolutely of one mind.

3. confront /kənˈfrʌnt/ (v.)

  • 含义: 面对、正视（问题/困难）
  • 例句: Children should be encouraged to slowly and carefully try to confront their terrifying experiences.

4. nightmare /ˈnaɪtmeər/ (n.)

  • 含义: 噩梦；极不愉快的经历
  • 例句: A child who has had a nightmare can find it very difficult to fall back asleep.

5. phenomenally /fɪˈnɒmɪnəli/ (adv.)

  • 含义: 惊人地、非凡地
  • 例句: Given a chance, children are phenomenally resilient.

6. resilient /rɪˈzɪliənt/ (adj.)

  • 含义: 有韧性的，能迅速恢复的
  • 例句: To be resilient is to be able to recover and come back from something difficult or bad.

7. trauma /ˈtrɔːmə/ (n.)

  • 含义: 创伤（心理或生理）
  • 例句: You can’t recover from war trauma if you’re living with continuing trauma.

8. PTSD /ˌpiː tiː es ˈdiː/ (abbr.) = Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder

  • 含义: 创伤后应激障碍
  • 例句: Fergal developed PTSD after years of reporting from war zones.

💬 跟读练习建议

  1. Shadowing（影子跟读）: 播放 YouTube 视频或 MP3，模仿 Neil 和 Becca 的语速、语调和停顿。特别注意 Neil 以标准英音（RP）发音，语速清晰适中，非常适合模仿。
  2. 分段练习: 将对话分成 5 个片段，每段反复听 3-5 遍，直到能跟随朗读。
  3. 角色扮演: 分别扮演 Neil、Becca 和 Fergal Keane 的角色，录制自己的声音后与原声对比。

🔄 即兴口语练习

练习 1: 观点表达

“Given a chance, the human spirit, and especially children, are phenomenally resilient.”

你同意 Fergal Keane 的这个观点吗？请用 2-3 分钟用英文发表你的看法。可以结合你个人的经历或身边观察到的例子。

练习 2: 角色改写

假设你是 BBC 记者，正在采访一位帮助战后儿童心理康复的心理学家。设计一个 1 分钟的英文对话（一问一答），内容包括：

  • 战争对儿童的主要心理影响
  • 他们采用什么康复方法
  • 孩子们展现出的韧性

练习 3: 词汇造句

使用今天学到的 3 个词汇（hyper-vigilant, confront, resilient）分别造一个与你自己生活相关的句子。

📋 发音技巧提示

1. 连读 (Linking)

  • “of one mind” → /əv wʌn maɪnd/ — “of” 的 /v/ 与 “one” 的 /w/ 自然连读
  • “absolutely of one mind” → 注意 “absolutely” 的重音在第一个音节 /ˈæbsəluːtli/

2. 重读与弱读 (Stress & Weak Forms)

  • “hyper-vigilant”: 重音在 HYP-er-VIG-i-lant — 注意两个重音
  • “phenomenally”: /fɪˈnɒmɪnəli/ — 重音在第二个音节 “NOM”
  • “resilient”: /rɪˈzɪliənt/ — 重音在第二个音节 “ZIL”
  • 助动词 “are” 在句子中弱读为 /ə/，不要重读

3. 特殊发音注意

  • “trauma”: /ˈtrɔːmə/ — 注意 “au” 发长音 /ɔː/
  • “nightmare”: /ˈnaɪtmeər/ — 结尾的 “re” 不发音的 /r/（英音），美音则卷舌
  • “confront”: /kənˈfrʌnt/ — 注意是 /frʌnt/ 不是 /frɔnt/
  • “PTSD”: 逐个字母读 /ˌpiː tiː es ˈdiː/

4. 语调练习

  • 提问句: “am I under threat?” → 升调，表现内心的不确定和焦虑
  • 强调句: “absolutely of one mind” → “absolutely” 用降调强调
  • Fergal Keane 的语速: 注意他以较慢、深思熟虑的节奏说话，适合跟读模仿
