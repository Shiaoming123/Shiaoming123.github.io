📝 英文原文（精华段落）

I’ve been testing out Gemini’s new task automation on the Pixel 10 Pro and the Galaxy S26 Ultra, which for the first time lets Gemini take the wheel and use apps for you. It’s limited to a small subset right now — a handful of food delivery and rideshare services — and it’s still in beta. It’s slow, it’s clunky at times, and it doesn’t solve any serious problem you had using your phone. But it’s impressive as hell, and I don’t think it’s hyperbole to say this is a glimpse of the future.

We’re still a long way off, but this is the first time I’ve seen a true AI assistant actually working on a phone — not in a keynote presentation or a carefully controlled demo inside a convention hall.

First off: Gemini is much slower than you, or me, or most anyone at using their phone. If you need to order an Uber right this second, you’re still the best person for the job. But task automation is designed to run in the background while you do other things on your phone. Even better, it keeps working while you’re not looking at your phone.

While it’s working, text appears at the bottom of the screen indicating what Gemini is doing. Stuff like “Selecting a second portion of Chicken Teriyaki for the combo,” which it did when I directed it to order my dinner. Watching Gemini figure things out on the fly honestly kinda rules.

Gemini is supposed to carry out your task right up to the point where it’s time to hit confirm and order your car or dinner so you can double-check its work. This is the only sane way to use this feature right now. In the tests I’ve run, I’ve never had it go rogue and finish my order for me. And it is surprisingly accurate; I’ve had to make very few adjustments to the final order.

Being able to use natural language when talking to the computer makes a huge difference when you’re controlling your smart home or placing your dinner order. If the computer is going to get tripped up and ask for clarification when you forget that the restaurant calls your meal a “plate” and not a “combo,” or if you ask for “slaw” instead of “shredded cabbage,” then it’s no more useful than the assistants we’ve been using for the past decade to set timers and play music.

💡 实用表达

  1. “I don’t think it’s hyperbole to say…” — 说某事毫不夸张。例：I don’t think it’s hyperbole to say this changes everything.
  2. “…right this second” — 立刻、马上。例：If you need it done right this second, do it yourself.
  3. “Figure things out on the fly” — 随机应变、临时处理。例：Good presenters can figure things out on the fly.
  4. “Go rogue” — 脱离控制、擅自行动。例：The software went rogue and deleted my files.
  5. “A stopgap solution” — 权宜之计。例：This is just a stopgap until we find a permanent fix.

🎯 练习步骤

  1. 通读全文：先完整读一遍英文原文，理解大意——AI 正从”语音助手”进化到”自主操作”阶段
  2. 精读标记：逐句阅读，重点关注科技类词汇（automation, beta, interface, confirm, adjust）
  3. 跟读练习：大声朗读 3 遍，注意科技报道的语调——描述问题时要自然停顿（“It’s slow, it’s clunky… But it’s impressive” 这种转折要读到位）
  4. 脱稿复述：合上文本，用自己的话复述 AI 助手目前能做到什么、还有哪些不足
  5. 结构化输出：
    • 这段话在说什么？——谷歌 Gemini AI 开始能帮用户在手机上自动完成点餐和叫车等任务
    • 主要观点是什么？ a) 目前速度较慢、功能有限，但方向正确 b) AI 能理解自然语言并在后台运行 c) 最终需要应用开发者适配 AI 而非 AI 适配人类应用
    • 我的感受/看法？——AI 助手终于从”听懂指令”进化到”替你做”了，虽然还慢但未来可期

🗣️ 口才小贴士

转折对比技巧（Contrast / Concession）：这篇报道大量使用了”先抑后扬”的修辞手法——先承认不足，再强调进步。这是英语口才中非常重要的对比结构：

“It’s slow, it’s clunky… But it’s impressive as hell…”

练习这个模式：

  1. 先列出所有缺点（用短词、排比）
  2. 用”But”或”That said”转折
  3. 给出积极的结论

模仿练习：

  • “The app is buggy, it’s expensive, and it crashes often. But when it works, it’s the best tool I’ve ever used.”
  • “He speaks slowly, he pauses a lot, and his accent is thick. But his ideas are the most insightful in the room.”

在日常对话中，先承认缺点再展示亮点，会让你的表达更有说服力和可信度。
