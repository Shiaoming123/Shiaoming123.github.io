📌 今日主题

脆弱的力量：为什么勇气需要脆弱——Brené Brown TED 演讲精华

📝 英文原文

I spent a decade studying human connection—what makes us feel connected, what gives us a sense of belonging, and why do we experience shame, fear, and vulnerability. The research led me to one surprising conclusion: vulnerability is not weakness. In fact, it is the most accurate measure of courage.

Here’s what I learned. People who embrace vulnerability are not people who fall apart in crisis. They are people who show up and say, “I want to be seen, even though there are no guarantees.” They are willing to say “I love you” first, to start a business, to have a difficult conversation, even when they’re terrified of the outcome.

The key to living a wholehearted life is the willingness to be imperfect. When we stop caring about what people think, we lose our capacity for connection. But when we’re willing to risk being vulnerable—sharing a creative idea, raising our hand in a meeting, or saying “I don’t know”—we open the door to authentic relationships and meaningful work.

Courage starts with showing up and letting ourselves be seen. It requires us to be honest about our feelings, to share our stories, and to connect with others in ways that might feel uncomfortable. That is the true definition of bravery.

💡 实用表达

  1. show up and let ourselves be seen — 出场并让自己被看见（勇敢展现真实自我）

    • 例：It takes courage to show up and let yourself be seen at work.

  2. no guarantees — 没有保证/没有确定性

    • 例：Starting a business means stepping into a world of no guarantees.

  3. willing to risk being vulnerable — 愿意冒险展现脆弱

    • 例：I’m willing to risk being vulnerable and share my honest opinion.

  4. the birthplace of innovation — 创新的发源地

    • 例：Failure is the birthplace of innovation and growth.

  5. embrace imperfection — 拥抱不完美

    • 例：We need to embrace imperfection if we want to be truly creative.

🗣️ 口才小贴士

演讲开场的黄金公式：用个人故事 + 悬念钩子

Brené Brown 的这段演讲之所以打动人，是因为她用”十年研究”作为钩子，然后用”一个出人意料的结论”制造悬念。当你需要做公开发言时，可以试试这个公式：

  1. 背景钩子：“我花了 [时间] 研究/了解…”
  2. 悬念转折：“但我发现了一个意想不到的事实…”
  3. 核心观点：直接抛出你的论点

例如：“我花了两年时间研究高效团队的共同点。但我发现，最重要的因素不是技能或经验——而是信任。”

这样的开头能瞬间抓住听众注意力，让他们想知道”为什么”。
